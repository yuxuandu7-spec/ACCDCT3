Tidal Garden
Documentation

Growth and flow at the edge of the sea.

I made Tidal Garden from two photos I took by the coast. I was interested in the holes in the rocks, the green patches around them, and the water moving through the gaps. I wanted to turn these small details into a changing network of light.

01 / Observe

Looking closer

The wide photo shows the waves meeting the shore. In the closer photo, I noticed that the rock surface is full of holes and connected channels. The green coverage leaves dark spaces between patches, while the water passes around the raised areas. These relationships became my starting point.

02 / Sketch

Tracing the movement

In my sketch, I used thin blue lines for crossing currents and thicker cyan lines for stronger waves. The green and dark shapes at the bottom describe the rock surface. Drawing helped me focus on movement, coverage, and empty space rather than copying the whole landscape.

03 / Connect

A network in the tide

I connected these observations to a growth system driven by a texture. The close photo becomes a resource field, so different areas support different amounts of growth. A feedback loop carries the previous state forward. I used this structure to explore a tidal growth network, with connected marks and spaces that keep changing.

Photograph→Resource field→Growth + feedback→Flowing light
04 / Make

Building the system

I used a Movie File In TOP to load the photo and GLSL TOPs for the resource field and Gray Scott growth. Feedback TOPs keep the previous frame. For the visible water-like lines, I layered noise and bent its coordinates to make flowing contours. Blur TOP and Composite TOP add a soft glow around the sharper lines.

05 / Adjust

Finding a lighter feeling

The first version felt too dark and green. I changed the colors to pale cyan, light blue, and yellow-green, then balanced the glow so the lines stayed clear. I also adjusted the noise distortion, line width, and speed. Keyboard In CHOP now switches between three strengths, with a short transition between them.

06 / Time + interaction

Three currents

Pressing 1, 2, or 3 in the TouchDesigner project changes the strength of the current. Calm moves slowly with finer lines. Swell keeps a steady movement. Surge moves much faster, with wider lines and stronger distortion. These short recordings show how each setting develops over time.
